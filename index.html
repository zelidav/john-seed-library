Rarity rating — how it's scored

Rarity is a 1–5 opinionated score of how hard a strain is to acquire once you want one. It's distinct from availability (which is the current retail status) and from valuation (which is estimated retail price). A strain can be low-rarity but sold out, or high-rarity but currently in stock.

Scoring factors (in order of weight)

  1. Breeder tier — primary signal. Reputation, production scale, and distribution control. Top-tier: A&B Company, Ethos Bx projects, Cult Classics hype drops, Cookies Fam S1 releases, Tiki Madman post-Cup winners.
  2. Seed type — S1 (self-pollinated feminized) and Bx (backcross) projects trade at collector premiums vs. regular feminized lines.
  3. Sealed status — an unopened pack preserves authentication (hologram, hand-label, breeder seal) and adds +1 to the score.
  4. Tester / freebie flags — testers were never retailed (+1 up to 4); freebies are bonus packs thrown in with other purchases and have reduced standalone value (-1).
  5. Strain-specific hype — named hype drops override the breeder tier. Examples in this library: Gushers S1, Cement Shoes S1, OGDLUX Bx3, Fresh Baked, Jurassic Kush (A&B pendant box).

Availability — current market status

Availability answers a different question than rarity: can you buy this today? A popular craft strain (high-rarity) may still be in stock if the breeder just did a drop. Conversely, a commercial line (low-rarity) may be discontinued.
